    Why a 30-day horizon

    • It fits the evidence. A change in guidance is absorbed by the market over several weeks, not days.
    • 14 days is too short for that to play out.
    • 60 days adds exposure without adding evidence, and would run close to the next earnings report, due in about 75 days.
    • One known event falls inside it: an FOMC decision on day 22.
    Base rate, higher close
    How often EXMP has finished a period of that length above where it started, whatever the news. Over 30 days it has done so 58% of the time. This is the benchmark: the thesis is only useful if it does better than this.
    Typical realized move
    The size of a normal move over that horizon, measured from past prices. Over 30 days EXMP has typically ended within about 5% of where it started, up or down. Use it to judge how far a strike is from spot.
    Current volatility
    EXMP has been moving about as much as it normally does, so the figures above are a fair guide to the next 30 days. In a high-volatility period they would understate the swings.
