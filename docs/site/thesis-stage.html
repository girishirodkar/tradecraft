The thesis stage

Stage 2 turns an underlying that has cleared the liquidity gate into a research brief: measured figures from its price history, and a researched view drafted by the agents. The user accepts, amends or rejects the view, or supplies their own. The thesis is then frozen before any option price is shown.

1. Overview

The output has two parts, produced in different ways and kept apart on the card. Measured figures are counted from price history in code and are the same every time. The researched view is drafted by the agents from the evidence. The view does not alter any measured figure.

ComponentDefinition
Measured figuresFor each of six horizons: how often the underlying has closed higher, and the usual range of its move. With them, the current volatility state.
ViewBullish, bearish, range-bound, or no view. A directional view is the agents' reading of the evidence. A range-bound view follows from a fixed rule.
Base rateHow often the view's outcome has occurred in the past at the primary horizon. Shown beside the view for reference. The system states no probability of its own for a view.
HorizonSix fixed periods from 7 to 180 days. One is designated primary and governs contract selection.
Resolution criterionThe observable outcome that determines whether the view was correct.
Invalidation conditionsWhat would show that the reason for the view has gone: each finding the view rests on, paired with what would reverse it. Based on evidence, not on price. The price-based risk exit is set separately, in stage 5.
EvidenceThe findings behind each claim, each traced to a stored source and graded for strength.

Every chart, table and diagram in parts 3 to 7 is explanation.

Procedure

  1. Receive the gate resultThe underlying, the screening outcome and the expiration ranges that carry tradeable contracts. No option prices are passed in.
  2. Retrieve the evidenceFilings, earnings materials, news, estimates, the earnings date and the macro calendar are fetched and stored with source and timestamp.
  3. Compute the quantitative inputsThe base rates, the usual ranges, the volatility state, and the descriptive price measures are calculated in code from price history.
  4. Analyse in parallelThree analyst agents read the evidence and the computed values and report findings independently.
  5. Draft the viewThe Thesis agent combines the findings into a bullish or bearish view with its reasoning, resolution criterion and invalidation conditions, or returns no view. It states no probability.
  6. Apply the fixed rulesCode places the base rate beside the view, applies the range-bound rule where there is no directional view, and recommends the primary horizon.
  7. ValidateA deterministic check confirms that the brief is internally consistent.
  8. User decisionThe user accepts, edits or rejects the view, or supplies a thesis of their own.
  9. FreezeThe thesis is locked and timestamped. It cannot be changed once option prices are revealed in stage 3.

Supply a thesisStop
  • The view and the figures are kept apart. The measured figures are arithmetic. The view is a reading of evidence. Showing them separately stops a persuasive write-up from being mistaken for better odds.
  • The system states no probability for its view. No evidence so far shows that the agents' view shifts the odds. The brief shows the base rate beside the view for reference, and nothing more, until the track record shows that the view deserves a figure of its own.
  • The label is permanent until earned. Every researched view is marked as having no demonstrated predictive edge until the track record shows otherwise.
  • No score is shown. A view with a resolution criterion can be checked; a 0 to 100 rating cannot.
  • The direction shapes the trade; later stages limit the cost of being wrong. A bullish or bearish view decides which way a structure is built. Costs, the critic's challenge and position sizing apply regardless.
  • Accepting a view is not approving a trade. It fixes the thesis to be tested. The critic's challenge follows in stage 4, before any sizing or order.

Agents

Five agents take part, arranged as a fan-out and a merge. The analysts do not debate one another: agents built on the same model tend to share the same errors, so agreement between them is weak evidence.

Measured figures

Three things are counted from the underlying's price history: how often it closed higher over each horizon, the usual range of its move, and its current volatility state. None involves a model or an agent.

The base rate

The base rate is the share of past periods of the horizon's length in which the underlying closed above its starting price. It is a count, not a model.

  1. Build the windowsFrom each day in the history, pair its close with the close 30 days on. Twenty years of daily data gives about 5,000 rolling windows.
  2. Count and divideThe share of windows in which the later close was higher. In the examples, 53%.
  3. Compute the margin of errorRolling windows overlap heavily, so the margin is computed from the number of non-overlapping windows: about 240 for a 30-day horizon, giving roughly ±3 points. Using the rolling count would overstate the precision tenfold.
  4. Blend with the average of all stocksA single stock's own rate is mostly chance, even over 20 years. It is blended with the average rate across all single stocks in the coverage universe. The longer the stock's history and the shorter the row, the more its own figure counts. The brief shows the blended rate, with the stock's own rate beside it.
Notes on the base rate, and sample size by horizon
  • Closes are adjusted for splits, not for dividends. A view resolves on the actual closing price, so the base rate is counted on the same basis.
  • The rate mostly reflects market drift. It carries no view about the company. Across the 105 underlyings measured on 16 November 2026 the 30-day rate had a median of 57%. That sample holds today's large companies, which flatters the figure.
  • Lookback. Up to 20 years where available, with a minimum of 5. Beyond 20 years the company is often a materially different business.
  • Stated uncertainty. The base rate is displayed with its range, for example 53% plus or minus 3.

  • Why blend. Measured on 17 November 2026 across 105 underlyings, the differences between stocks' base rates were only slightly larger than chance alone would produce. A stock's own 63% is weak evidence that it rises more often than others.
  • An example. A stock with 20 years of history and an own 30-day rate of 63% shows about 59%. A stock with 5 years and an own rate of 46% shows about 55%.
  • Index funds and ETFs are not blended. They behave differently from single stocks and most have long histories, so their own rate is used. XSP, with under six years, uses the history of SPX, which tracks the same index.

  • The weights are provisional. Stocks move together, which makes the differences between them look smaller than they are, so the weights lean somewhat towards the average.
  • The stated range. The plus-or-minus figure is that of the stock's own history, which is the wider and more cautious of the two.

The usual range

For each horizon, the usual range is the band, either side of the starting price, that contained 68% of past moves of that length. A usual range of ±5% over 30 days means that in about two periods out of three the close finished within 5% of where it started.

Why a band, and what it is used for
  • Why a band and not a standard deviation. A standard deviation is distorted by crisis periods. Measured on 16 November 2026, one bank's 30-day figure came to ±13.5% by standard deviation and ±9.1% by the 68% band, because 2008 dominates the former. The band holds 68% of past moves for every underlying by construction.
  • It is not adjusted for the volatility state. Scaling the range for the state was tested on unseen data on 16 November 2026 and was no more accurate than the unadjusted range. The adjustment was dropped.
  • Its uses. It defines the range-bound resolution criterion, gives the user a sense of scale, and informs strike selection in stage 3.

Price description

What was measured, and how technical analysis is used
  • It was measured and found wanting. Across 105 underlyings and 20 years, being above the average shifted the chance of closing higher by less than a point, in the wrong direction for a momentum reading. A stock's own trend effect in the first half of its history barely predicted its effect in the second.
  • No alternative indicator will be searched for. Testing several and keeping the best would overstate its value.
  • No price-forecasting models. Statistical models that project future prices from past prices are not used.
  • Timeframes are reported side by side. They are not combined into an agreement count, because they derive from the same price series and are not independent.
  • The chart is always drawn, with the levels marked, so the user can see what the thesis refers to.
  • The heavier use comes later. Chart levels are shown beside each candidate in stage 3 as context, and each candidate's position relative to them is recorded so that their value can be measured later.

4. How a view is formed

A directional view is the agents' reading of what the evidence says about the company over the primary horizon. It is a judgement, drafted for the user to accept, amend or reject. It is not a calculation.

Directional view: single names

What may support a direction

Filings and earnings materials. Consensus estimates and their recent revisions. Company news. Sector and macro context. Each claim cites a stored item of evidence and carries a grade.

What may not

Price trend, momentum and relative strength, which are description only. Support and resistance, which are description. The underlying's option prices and implied volatility, which this stage does not see.

How the findings are combined

The Thesis agent does not weight the three analysts by percentage. Any such split would be an invented number, and weighted inputs add up to a score, which this system does not produce. The findings are combined by an order of precedence.

AnalystSay on the direction
Fundamental AnalystDecides whether there is a view, and which way. Company-specific evidence leads
Regime AssessorQualifies the view. Sector and macro findings may support or oppose it, but may not start one. Index instruments differ; see the next section
Technical AnalystNone. It supplies a description of price behaviour and chart levels
  1. Company evidence leadsA directional view requires at least one company-specific finding from the Fundamental Analyst: a change in guidance, a revision to estimates, or a material filing or news item.
  2. Sector and macro cannot start a viewThey may support or oppose a view that company evidence has established. A favourable sector alone is not a reason to be bullish on an underlying, and a weak one alone is not a reason to be bearish.
  3. Conflicting company evidence means no viewRaised guidance beside a newly filed share offering is mixed evidence. The agent does not choose between them.
  4. Opposition from the backdrop is shown, not nettedWhere company evidence points one way and the sector or macro backdrop the other, the view stands, the opposing evidence is listed, and the view is marked as contested.
  5. Every finding carries a grade, and a view needs solid evidenceEach finding is graded strong, moderate or weak by the rule below. A directional view requires at least one company-specific finding graded moderate or strong.

Evidence grades

A grade says how reliable a finding is: whether it is current and comes from the source itself. It makes no claim about how well that kind of evidence predicts direction. Grades are set by three checks applied in code, so that the agents do not grade their own work.

The three checks

CheckPassed when
Primary sourceIt comes from a company filing, an earnings release or official data, not from a report about them
RecentIt is dated within the last 30 days, or since the latest earnings report
SpecificIt concerns this company, or for an index the index itself, not the sector or the market in general

Checks passedGrade
All threeStrong

Guidance raised in last week's earnings release is strong. A two-month-old article about sector demand is weak.

What grades are used for, and their limit
  • They define thin evidence. A view needs at least one company-specific finding graded moderate or strong. For an index, the finding must come from its lead evidence.
  • They order the evidence. The brief lists the strongest findings first.
  • They do nothing else. Grades are not added up, are not turned into a score, and change no figure.
  • A grade is not a measure of importance. A finding can be current and well sourced and still be trivial, such as a routine filing. Judging what matters remains with the agents.
  • The 30-day window is a provisional design choice.
  • No view is a normal result. Where the evidence is thin or mixed, the agents return no view. Thin means that every company-specific finding is graded weak; mixed means that the company evidence conflicts. A system obliged to produce a view on every underlying would manufacture them.
  • The second rule has a cost. The system will not turn bearish on an underlying solely because its sector is deteriorating. That is consistent with this project's research, which rejected sector rotation as a signal and found that macro events should generate no entries.
  • Evidence is counted by family. The families are fundamentals and expectations, news and events, and sector and macro. A view supported by one family is marked as weakly supported. Evidence that opposes the view is listed beside the evidence that supports it.
  • How a view earns a probability of its own. Whether an agent's reading of public information predicts direction cannot be tested on past data, because the model may have seen that period. It can only be tested going forward. Every view is logged and resolved at its horizon. Once about 100 directional views have resolved, their hit rate is compared with the base rate. Only if the views are right more often than the base rate may a view carry a probability above the base rate, and then by the measured amount.
  • A bearish view runs against the base rate. Most underlyings close lower less than half the time over any horizon. The brief states that figure and leaves the judgement to the user.

Directional view: index instruments

An index has no company-specific evidence, so the single-name rules cannot be applied to it as written. Each index instrument instead has lead evidence, which may start a view, and qualifying evidence, which may only support or oppose one.

  2. Qualifying evidence cannot start a viewIt may support or oppose one that lead evidence has established.
  3. Conflicting lead evidence means no viewWhere aggregate earnings point one way and macro and policy the other, the result is no view. Neither takes precedence.

Notes on the index logic
  • Macro and policy may start a view on the equity indices. Decided on 17 November 2026, so that index views are available throughout the year and not only around earnings season. This project's research found macro events the least reliable basis for direction, which is one more reason the view carries no claimed edge and is scored against the base rate.
  • The analysts' roles shift. The Fundamental Analyst covers aggregate earnings and the largest holdings. The Regime Assessor leads on macro and policy for the equity indices and on rates and policy for TLT. No additional agent is required.
  • Bearish index views run firmly against the base rate. Indices have closed higher more often than single stocks, and the brief states the figure.
  • Everything else is unchanged. The measured figures, the base rate shown beside the view, the label, the scoring and the range-bound rule apply as for single names. The index flag list applies in place of the single-name list.

The range-bound view

A range-bound view is set by rule, not by the agents. All four conditions must hold.

  • The agents have returned no directional view.
  • The underlying is in a quiet volatility state.
  • The row is the 7-day or the 14-day row.
  • No earnings date or flagged event falls inside the row.
Why two rows, and why it is mostly context
  • Why only the two short rows. On unseen data the quiet state lifted the share of closes inside the usual range by about 7 points over 7 days and 5 over 14. Over 30 days the lift was about 3 points, too small to rest a view on.
  • It is context far more often than a trade. Quiet underlyings and liquid short-dated options seldom coincide. In the 16 November 2026 snapshot, 25 of 106 underlyings had two eligible calls and two eligible puts expiring in 7 to 14 days at the default settings, and none of the three underlyings then in a quiet state was among them.
  • It is not a trade signal. A quiet underlying usually has inexpensive options. Whether a range-bound structure is worth taking depends on option prices, which this stage does not see.
  • Range-bound structures mostly sell premium. This project's tests found no excess return from rule-based premium selling on indices after 2012. That context is shown on the brief, and sizing permits defined-risk structures only.
  • An active state creates no view. It is reported as a note that larger moves are more likely.
  • Reviewed against outcomes. Every range-bound row is scored. The rule is reviewed once about 100 have resolved.

When there is no view

No view is expected to be a frequent result. The brief is still produced: the measured figures, the evidence and any risk flags are shown, with the reason no view was formed.

KindCause
Nothing foundThe evidence is thin or mixed, and the conditions for a range-bound view are not met
DeclinedA severe flag stopped the view: a going-concern warning or an announced takeover
Insufficient dataUnder five years of price history, or a required input is missing

The user then has two options.

Stop

The workflow ends and the user returns to the home page of the application. Nothing is written to the permanent record: no decision records and no scoring entry.

Supply a thesis

The user enters a thesis as free text. The system evaluates it and reports on its credibility, as set out below. After a declined result this is an override: the flag stays attached and a decision record is written.

  • What stopping gives up. With nothing recorded, the system cannot later report how often it returned no view, or check how the underlyings it passed on went on to behave.
  • The agents are not asked again for a different answer. A view obtained by repeated asking is not evidence. The user may submit the same underlying on another day; the measured figures are deterministic, so a different result means the data has changed.

Evaluating a thesis the user supplies

A supplied thesis is checked claim by claim against the evidence already retrieved. The result is a statement of what the evidence supports. It is not an endorsement and carries no score.

  1. Read the textThe system identifies the direction, the time frame and each factual claim the thesis makes.
  2. Check each claimEach claim is marked supported, contradicted or not found, with the stored item of evidence cited.
  3. Check for rejected signalsIf the thesis rests on a signal this project's research excluded, such as unusual options flow or social-media sentiment, the system says so.
  4. Show what cuts against itOpposing evidence, the base rate for the stated direction, and any risk flags are set beside the thesis.
  5. State a verdict in wordsOne of four: well supported, partly supported, not supported, or rests on a rejected signal.
  6. Confirm the termsThe system extracts the direction, the horizon and at least one invalidation condition from the text, and the user confirms or corrects them. An invalidation condition, meaning what would show the thesis to be wrong, is required before the thesis can be frozen.
  • The guard against agreement. A system asked to assess a user's idea tends to find reasons to agree with it. Checking claims one at a time against stored evidence, and always showing the opposing evidence, is the safeguard. The critic's challenge in stage 4 follows as for any thesis.
  • The verdict follows a fixed rule. A claim counts as supported only when the evidence behind it is graded moderate or strong. No supported claim and at least one rejected signal: rests on a rejected signal. No supported claim: not supported. At least one supported claim and at least one other that is contradicted, not found or a rejected signal: partly supported. Every claim supported: well supported. One contradicted claim cannot be outvoted.
  • The verdict does not gate the workflow. The user may proceed with a thesis the evidence does not support. The verdict and the user's decision are both recorded.
  • The same path serves every user-supplied thesis, including one entered in place of a view the user has rejected.

Horizon and resolution criterion

A view always refers to one specific, observable outcome over a stated period. Both are fixed before any option price is shown.

Horizons

Figures are stated at six fixed horizons, weighted towards the periods that matter for options trading: 7, 14 and 30 days (short), 60 and 90 days (medium), and 180 days (long, as context). Fixed horizons make base rates comparable. One is designated primary. The system recommends it by a fixed rule, and the user may choose a different row.

Resolution criterion

One standard outcome per view. Bullish: the underlying closes above the reference close at the horizon. Bearish: it closes below. Range-bound: it closes within the usual range either side of the reference close.

The outcome cannot be known in advance. On the horizon date the system compares the closing price with the criterion recorded on day one and marks the view correct or incorrect. That comparison is mechanical and involves no judgement.

Recommending the primary horizon

Where there is a directional view, the system recommends one row as primary. The recommendation is a fixed rule applied in code, so the same inputs always produce the same recommendation. It takes no account of how strong any row looks.

  1. Exclude the 180-day rowIt is context only and is never primary.
  2. Exclude rows without eligible contractsThe liquidity screen reports which expiry ranges carry eligible contracts. A row is kept only if contracts expiring on or after its horizon date are available. This uses counts only; no option price is read.
  3. Set aside rows that span a scheduled eventWhere an earnings date or a flagged binary event falls inside a row, rows that end before it are preferred. If every remaining row spans the event, none is set aside and the recommendation carries a warning.
  4. Take the row nearest 30 daysAmong the rows that remain, the one closest to 30 days is recommended.

How the recommendation is used
  • The reason is shown. The brief states in one line why the row was recommended.
  • The user decides. Any other row may be selected as primary. The recommendation and the user's selection are each written as a decision record.
  • Range-bound views. Only the 7- and 14-day rows are candidates, and the same rule applies to those two.
  • When no row qualifies. If no row has eligible contracts, no recommendation is made and the brief says so. When the market is closed and no snapshot covers the underlying, step 2 is skipped and the recommendation is marked provisional.
  • All six rows are scored whichever is primary, so nothing is gained by selecting a row afterwards.

5. Controls

Four mechanisms keep the brief within what the evidence supports: risk flags, safeguards on the view, a validation check, and a list of what the stage may not use.

Filings, earnings materials, news, sector and macro context are read for a defined list of risk flags. An agent decides whether a flag is present and cites the evidence. It does not create new flags, and a flag changes no figure on the brief.

What a flag does

Adds an invalidation condition. A ruling due on day 22 becomes a stated exit condition. Reduces position size. A flagged thesis is marked as carrying event risk and is sized down in stage 5. Stops the thesis in severe cases. A going-concern warning or an announced merger leads to no view.

What a flag does not do

It does not change any figure on the brief. Every flag is logged with the thesis. Once enough theses have resolved, flagged and unflagged theses are compared, and a deduction is introduced only for a flag type that is shown to predict worse outcomes, at the measured size.

  • Triggers are set so that a flag is the exception. A flag that is nearly always raised says nothing. Inflation and employment reports are each monthly, so any 14-day period contains one; they are flagged for indices on the 7-day horizon only. Monthly long-bond auctions and index rebalances are not flagged.
  • Every scheduled release is still shown. For an index, the brief lists each Fed decision, inflation release and employment report inside the primary horizon, with its date and with the index's usual move on past release days beside its usual move on an ordinary day. This is counted in code from price history and the release calendar.
  • One size reduction, however many flags. Several size-reducing flags on the same thesis produce a single reduction. They do not compound.
  • Direction matters. A flag reduces size only where it works against the view or raises the likelihood of a large move in either direction. A guidance reduction counts against a bullish thesis and not against a bearish one.
  • Flags removed on review. Restatement and trading halt (little relevance to liquid underlyings); insider selling (not supported by this project's research); margin deterioration and revenue shortfall (already reflected in guidance and in estimate revisions); sector trend opposing the view (price-derived, and already shown as description).
  • Thresholds are provisional. The 90-day, 14-day and 7-day windows and the 20% earnings threshold are design choices.
Why flags do not deduct points

Deducting points for flags was considered and rejected on 16 November 2026, for five reasons.

  • The base rate already contains these risks. Twenty years of history include litigation, dilution and weak quarters. Their average effect is in the base rate, and a deduction would count it twice.
  • The information is public. A filed registration or a known court date is visible to the market and largely reflected in the price.
  • Flags are common. Most annual reports disclose litigation, and a 30-day horizon almost always contains an inflation release or a central bank meeting. Routine deductions would lower every figure.
  • The design would be asymmetric. Evidence could raise a figure by a few points and lower it by many, so bullish theses would be systematically understated.
  • Many flags concern risk, not direction. A pending ruling makes a large move in either direction more likely. Smaller position size is the appropriate response to that.

Safeguards

More inputs make a view read as more convincing. They do not necessarily make it more accurate. Four safeguards keep the stated confidence in line with the evidence.

The agents set no figures

The base rate shown beside a view is counted in code. However persuasive the reasoning, no figure on the brief changes.

Evidence families

Evidence is grouped as fundamentals and expectations, news and events, and sector and macro. A view supported by one family is marked as weakly supported. Price-derived measures are description and are not counted.

Opposing evidence is shown

Evidence against the view is listed beside the evidence for it, with the same grading. A brief that shows only support is rejected by the validation check.

The view is scored against the base rate

Every view is resolved at its horizon. If directional views are not right more often than the base rate once about 100 have resolved, the system reports that the views add no predictive value.

Validation before the user sees a draft

There is no critic agent in this stage. The critic operates in stage 4, on a different model. A second critic here would duplicate that work and add assurance without adding accuracy. A deterministic check runs on every draft instead.

  • Every claim cites a stored item of evidence, and every finding carries a grade set by the three checks.
  • Every figure in the thesis matches the computed value it cites.
  • The figure shown beside the view equals the base rate of its outcome at the primary horizon, and the draft states no other probability.
  • A directional view cites at least one company-specific finding graded moderate or strong, or for an index one such finding from its lead evidence, and that evidence does not conflict with itself.
  • A resolution criterion is present, and each finding the view rests on is paired with an evidence-based invalidation condition. No invalidation condition is a price level.
  • An earnings date inside the horizon is flagged, and the view is marked dated or undated.
  • No price-derived measure is cited in support of a direction.
  • Evidence opposing the view is listed, or the brief states that none was found.
  • Every risk flag raised is on the defined list and cites its evidence.
  • A range-bound view meets all four of its conditions.
  • The evidence families are listed, and a view supported by one family is marked as weakly supported.

A draft that fails is returned to the Thesis agent once. If it fails again, the user is shown the draft together with the failed checks.

What this stage does not use

  • The underlying's option data. Option prices, implied volatility and market-implied probabilities are withheld. Stage 4 sets the brief's figures beside the market's, and that comparison would be circular if the view had been formed with sight of the market's figures.
  • Rejected signals. Social-media sentiment, unusual options flow and similar inputs were examined in research and excluded.
  • Index-level VIX is the one exception. It describes the market regime, not the underlying's own options.

6. Decision and record

User decision and freeze

System-drafted view

The default path. The user may accept the view, edit any component, or reject it. The original draft is retained beside any edit. An edit to the direction is recorded as the user's.

User-supplied thesis

The user may bypass the draft and enter a thesis as free text. The system evaluates its credibility claim by claim, then asks the user to confirm the direction, horizon and invalidation conditions. The same record is written.

  • Optional independent estimate. Before the draft is shown, the user may record their own estimate of the chance that the underlying closes higher. Reading a draft first anchors judgement on it, so the two figures are stored separately.
  • Freeze. Once accepted, the thesis is locked and timestamped. It cannot be revised after option prices are shown in stage 3.
  • Scoring. Every view is logged and resolved at its horizon, whether or not a trade follows. No track-record claim is displayed before 100 views have resolved.

What is handed to stage 3

The brief the user reads is kept short. The record passed to the later stages is complete. A longer write-up would not help stage 3, which works from fields, and would make a view read as stronger than the evidence warrants.

Fields in the record

The underlying, the view and whether it is contested or weakly supported. The primary horizon. The resolution criterion and invalidation conditions. The base rates, usual ranges and volatility state. Scheduled events with dates. Risk flags and their effects. Catalyst timing. Chart levels, shown as context in stage 3.

Catalyst timing

Whether the view depends on a dated event, and when. A view that turns on a decision expected on day 12 calls for a different expiry from one that rests on estimates which have already risen. The Thesis agent marks the view dated, with the date and the finding it comes from, or undated.

  • The complete evidence set travels with the record. It includes findings not shown on the brief. The critic in stage 4 checks the evidence itself, not a summary of it.
  • Nothing about option prices is in the record. Stage 3 opens the option chain for the first time.
  • Fields added for the challenge in stage 4. Each finding records its evidence family and the underlying's price move since the finding became public. Each invalidation condition records its type, from a fixed list, so that the system can tell whether the condition can be watched. The record lists every input that was unavailable, with the reason.

Decision records

Each decision in this stage is written to the decision record at the time it is made: the evidence retrieved, the analysts' findings, the measured figures, the drafted view, the validation result, the horizon recommendation, and the user's acceptance, edit or rejection. Where a trade follows, the chain is retained permanently and linked to that trade.

7. Background

How the stage came to its present design: what was measured, and the alternatives that were set aside.

Show the measurements and the rejected alternatives

What was measured on 16 November 2026

This stage was first designed to compute a direction from price trend and estimate revisions. Four measurements on 20 years of daily prices led to the present design.

QuestionFindingConsequence
Does price trend shift the chance of closing higher?By under a point, in the wrong direction, and not consistently for any one stockTrend is description only
Could estimate revisions alone carry a direction?A bearish reading was reachable on about 13% of underlyings. The frequency of revisions could not be measuredNo rule-based direction

With the computed direction removed, fewer than one underlying in ten would have carried any system view. The researched view restores a drafted direction without claiming that it improves the odds.

Considered and rejected

AlternativeWhy it was not adopted

Open design questions. Not yet settled: the choice of data provider, and the detailed design of the Monitor agent that watches invalidation conditions while a position is open. The stage takes one underlying per request; handling a list is out of scope for now. The volatility-state thresholds, the flag thresholds and the base-rate blending weights are provisional design choices.

Limitations. The research behind this design found little evidence that the direction of a liquid underlying can be predicted after costs, and the measurements of 16 November 2026 agree. This stage is designed to produce an honest, falsifiable and auditable brief. The researched view is not presented as a source of predictive edge.
