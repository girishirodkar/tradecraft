# Research Strand 04 — Alternative Data, NLP, and Signal Decay
**Prepared:** 10 November 2026
**Scope:** EDGAR/filing text analysis, news sentiment, social-media sentiment, signal decay, data obtainability as of late 2026, practical NLP engineering.
**Out of scope (other strands):** competitor products, technical-analysis literature, options pricing/flow mechanics, creator ecosystem.

---

## 0. Executive judgment up front

Three conclusions dominate everything below, and they are uncomfortable for the product thesis:

1. **The academically documented effect sizes for every signal in this strand are small — tens of basis points over days to weeks — and were measured on equity returns, not on options.** Equity signals of this size do not reliably survive the bid-ask spread on a single-name option contract, let alone a round trip. A signal worth 30 bps/month on a stock is economically invisible once routed through a contract with a 3–8% spread.
2. **Post-publication decay is the rule, not the exception.** McLean & Pontiff's 58% post-publication shrinkage is the central number of this report. Several of the specific signals the app wants to use (insider cluster buys, LLM news sentiment, 13F cloning) show direct in-sample evidence of decay or regime concentration.
3. **Data obtainability has deteriorated sharply in 2026 and is still deteriorating.** Reddit announced on 30 September 2026 that it is closing the public Data API entirely (new access requests stop 31 October 2026; full shutdown March 2027). Stocktwits' developer API is closed to new registrations. X API costs $200/month minimum for ~15k reads. **Social-media scraping as a product input is, as of this month, mostly foreclosed for a small B2C app unless it buys a license or an aggregator.** SEC EDGAR, by contrast, remains free, generous, and viable.

A defensible product takes EDGAR seriously, treats news sentiment as a weak feature, and treats social sentiment as a *display* feature (what the crowd is saying) rather than a *scoring* feature (what the crowd implies about returns).

## 1. EDGAR / Filing text analysis

### 1.1 Finance-specific dictionaries vs. general-purpose dictionaries

**CLAIM:** General-purpose psychology dictionaries systematically misclassify financial text — roughly three-quarters of Harvard-dictionary "negative" word occurrences in 10-Ks are words that are not negative in a financial context.
**SOURCE:** Loughran, T. & McDonald, B., "When Is a Liability Not a Liability? Textual Analysis, Dictionaries, and 10-Ks," *Journal of Finance* 66(1), 2011. [PDF copy](https://www.uts.edu.au/globalassets/sites/default/files/adg_cons2015_loughran-mcdonald-je-2011.pdf) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1331573) · peer-reviewed.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong. The 73.8% misclassification figure is directly measured; words like *tax*, *cost*, *capital*, *liability*, *board*, *vice* drive it. Some misclassified words (*mine*, *cancer*, *crude*) load on specific industries and create spurious correlations.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Never use VADER, TextBlob, NLTK's default lexicon, or a generic LLM "is this positive or negative" prompt on filing text without domain grounding. The LM word lists (Fin-Neg, Fin-Pos, Uncertainty, Litigious, Constraining, Strong/Weak Modal) are the floor, not the ceiling. This is also a cheap credibility win for the product: it is a real methodological choice you can defend.

**CLAIM:** The return predictability from 10-K negative-word proportion is statistically real but economically tiny, and a long/short strategy built on it produced no significant alpha in the original paper.
**SOURCE:** Loughran & McDonald 2011, as above. Fin-Neg proportion coefficient on 4-day filing-period excess return: −19.538 (t = −2.64); average regression R² ≈ 2.4–2.6%. The authors' own framing is that textual analysis is not the key to returns.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong (and strongly negative for trading use).
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Filing tone belongs in the score with a **small** weight, if at all. The honest framing to the user is "this filing reads unusually negative" as context, not "this filing implies a bearish trade."

**CLAIM:** Term weighting (tf-idf) largely rescues the general-purpose dictionary, which means the dictionary-choice debate is partly a noise-weighting debate.
**SOURCE:** Loughran & McDonald 2011 — with tf-idf weighting, both Fin-Neg and Harvard H4N-Inf produce nearly identical significant coefficients (≈ −0.003, t ≈ −3.1 to −3.2).
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** If you implement a dictionary pipeline, implement tf-idf weighting. It matters more than the lexicon choice.

### 1.2 Changes in filing language ("Lazy Prices")

**CLAIM:** Year-over-year *changes* in 10-K/10-Q language predict negative future returns, with a long non-changers / short changers five-factor alpha reported up to 188 bp/month in the strongest section cut, and 30–50 bp/month in the paper's headline framing.
**SOURCE:** Cohen, L., Malloy, C. & Nguyen, Q., "Lazy Prices," *Journal of Finance* 75(3), 2020, DOI [10.1111/jofi.12885](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jofi.12885). Working paper: [SSRN 1658471](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1658471). Full text via [Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/576a041c-19c4-47d8-b28d-2a5ef079f54f). Peer-reviewed.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong for the effect's existence; Moderate for the headline magnitude, because the paper reports a wide band (equal-weighted L/S 18–45 bp/month; value-weighted up to 58 bp/month, t = 3.59, ≈7%/yr; the 188 bp figure is the Item 1A Risk Factors subset with t = 2.76).
**Details worth keeping:** Sample 1995–2014, full filer universe. Risk Factors (Item 1A) changes are the strongest predictor; Legal Proceedings changes predict −71 bp/month (t = 3.29); MD&A (Item 7) is significant but smaller (11–22 bp/month). About 86% of changes are negative in sentiment; the 14% positive changes predict positive returns. Drift builds over ~6 months and persists to ~18 months without reversal. No announcement-day return, consistent with inattention rather than overreaction. Changes also predict lower future operating income, net income, sales, more negative earnings surprises, and bankruptcies.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** **This is the single best-supported filing signal for the product**, and it is directly computable from EDGAR at zero data cost. It is also *mechanically* well suited to a 1–6 month options horizon rather than a weekly one — which should discipline the strategy selection module. Caveats: (a) the value-weighted magnitude (~7%/yr gross) is the honest number for liquid optionable names, not 22%/yr; (b) it needs two comparable filings, so it is unavailable for recent IPOs and for firms that change filing structure; (c) it is a 6–18 month drift signal, which argues for longer-dated contracts, not weeklies.

**CLAIM:** The "moving targets" variant — firms quietly dropping previously emphasized metrics from earnings calls — is detectable and an LLM extractor roughly doubles the risk-adjusted spread versus a rule-based NER baseline, but the underlying Fama-MacBeth coefficient is not statistically significant.
**SOURCE:** Choi et al., "From Text to Alpha: Can LLMs Track Evolving Signals in Corporate Disclosures?", arXiv [2510.03195](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03195), 2025. Preprint, not peer-reviewed.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Weak. Q5–Q1 spread −0.52% 5-factor alpha (t = −2.55) for the LLM method vs −0.14% (t = −0.70) for NER, but the Fama-MacBeth coefficient is −0.0370 with t = −0.95 — insignificant — and the paper's own conclusion overstates significance relative to its table. Sample limited to S&P 100 (cost constraint), 2010–2024, 5,615 firm-quarters.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Interesting direction; not a production signal. Also a useful cautionary example: the LLM-versus-baseline improvement was demonstrated on a 100-stock universe because running the LLM over a wider universe was unaffordable. That cost wall will hit this product too.

### 1.3 Readability, tone and length

**CLAIM:** The Fog index is invalid for business documents; 10-K complete-submission **file size** is a better and far cheaper proxy for disclosure complexity.
**SOURCE:** Loughran, T. & McDonald, B., "Measuring Readability in Financial Disclosures," *Journal of Finance* 69(4), 2014, pp. 1643–1671. [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1111/jofi.12162) · [Harvard Corp Gov summary](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2014/06/24/measuring-readability-in-financial-disclosures/). Peer-reviewed.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong. Complex-word counts in 10-Ks are dominated by ordinary business terms (*company*, *operations*, *management*); sentence-boundary detection is unreliable in filings. Larger file size predicts higher post-filing abnormal return volatility, higher absolute SUE, and higher analyst dispersion.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** 10-K file size is a **volatility** predictor, not a direction predictor. That is actually more useful for an options product than for an equity one: it argues for feeding file size (and its year-over-year change) into implied-vs-realized-vol reasoning and strategy selection (debit vs credit structures), not into the bullish/bearish classifier.

**CLAIM:** The readability literature is internally contradictory and sensitive to measurement choices.
**SOURCE:** Survey — "Text analysis in financial disclosures," arXiv [2101.04480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.04480). Preprint survey. It documents that Li (2008) found loss-making firms write harder filings, while Lo, Ramos & Rogo found firms *beating* prior earnings write less readable filings — opposite signs. Siano & Wysocki show numeric content drives much of the readability-performance link.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate (survey-level).
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Do not build a readability-based directional feature. The sign is contested.

**CLAIM:** Published filing-text alphas in the survey literature are reported at levels (e.g. ~10%/yr four-factor alpha for a risk-sentiment long/short) that have not been subjected to formal replication, and most studies do not report classifier accuracy.
**SOURCE:** arXiv [2101.04480](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.04480), which notes explicitly that few papers report the accuracy of their classification methodology.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Treat any single-paper filing-text alpha as unverified. Build your own holdout.

### 1.4 8-K event studies

**CLAIM:** Specific 8-K item types carry measurable, persistent negative abnormal returns — Item 4.02 (non-reliance / restatement) filings show mean CAR of about −0.87% at +1 day widening to −1.54% at +20 days (−1.10% and −2.00% excluding 2021).
**SOURCE:** sec-api.io (Data2Value GmbH), ["Stock Price Reactions to Item 4.02 Disclosures in SEC Form 8-K Filings"](https://sec-api.io/resources/stock-price-reactions-to-item-4-02-disclosures-in-sec-form-8-k-filings). **Vendor analysis, not peer-reviewed.** 8,143 filings 2004–2023; event study 2007–2023; CAR computed as filer return minus SPY, described as a market model but implemented as market-adjusted; no estimation window or beta described; significance test not explained. 64% of filings post after close, with no stated timing adjustment.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Weak as executed (method underspecified, vendor-produced), but directionally consistent with the peer-reviewed restatement literature.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** 8-K *item-type* classification is a high-value, low-cost feature and is cleanly obtainable from EDGAR. Items 4.02 (non-reliance), 5.02 (officer departure), 1.01/2.01 (material agreements/acquisitions), 3.01 (listing deficiency) are the ones worth treating as events. But the signal is an **event flag**, not a sentiment score — and the drift is ~1–2% over 20 days, which is a plausible options play only on cheap contracts.
**Gap:** I could not retrieve Lerman & Livnat's "The New Form 8-K Disclosures" (*Review of Accounting Studies* 15(4), 2010) — the NYU archive copy is robots-disallowed and the search budget was exhausted. That is the canonical peer-reviewed 8-K information-content paper and should be read before building this feature. [RePEc record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v15y2010i4d10.1007_s11142-009-9114-7.html).

### 1.5 Insider transactions (Form 4)

**CLAIM:** Insider purchases predict positive abnormal returns, with the canonical estimate at 52–68 bp/month over six months, concentrated in purchases (not sales) and in smaller firms.
**SOURCE:** Jeng, Metrick & Zeckhauser, "Estimating the Returns to Insider Trading: A Performance-Evaluation Perspective," *Review of Economics and Statistics*, 2003 (sample 1975–1996); Lakonishok & Lee, "Are Insider Trades Informative?", *Review of Financial Studies* 14(1), 2001 (sample 1975–1995). Figures as summarized by [2iQ Research](https://www.2iqresearch.com/blog/profiting-from-insider-transactions-a-review-of-the-academic-research) — **a vendor that sells insider data**; the underlying papers are peer-reviewed but I was summarizing through the vendor's table.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate. The underlying papers are strong; my access to the numbers is second-hand through an interested party.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Purchases only. Weight small-caps higher. But see decay below.

**CLAIM:** Only "opportunistic" insider trades carry information; routine, calendar-predictable trades carry none — and routine trades are over half of all insider trades.
**SOURCE:** Cohen, L., Malloy, C. & Pomorski, L., "Decoding Inside Information," *Journal of Finance* 67(3), 2012, pp. 1009–1043. [NBER w16454](https://www.nber.org/papers/w16454) · [AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Decoding-Inside-Information). Peer-reviewed. Opportunistic trades: 82 bp/month value-weighted abnormal return; routine traders ≈ zero.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** **A naive "insider bought" flag is close to worthless.** You must classify each insider's trade history to separate routine from opportunistic. This requires multi-year Form 4 history per insider — buildable from EDGAR bulk data, but a real engineering commitment. If you cannot do the classification, do not ship the feature.

**CLAIM (NEGATIVE / DISCONFIRMING):** The tradable insider cluster-buy edge in the modern, post-2006 electronic-filing era appears concentrated almost entirely in 2008–2009 and is slightly negative after 2017.
**SOURCE:** "The insider-buying edge was mostly 2008," [donttrade Substack](https://donttrade.substack.com/p/the-insider-buying-edge-was-mostly). **Independent blog backtest, not peer-reviewed.** Method: all 81 quarterly SEC Form 4 bulk files 2006Q1–2026Q1, ~470,754 filing-owner records, open-market purchases only; cluster = ≥2 distinct insiders buying the same stock in one calendar week totalling ≥$100,000; 21-trading-day returns from the close after the last filing, relative to SPY. Findings: 2008–09 supply ~94% of the excess-return sum for 3+ insider clusters; excluding 2008–09, no contiguous early-era window is significantly positive; post-2017 tradable average ≈ −0.20% at 2+ insiders, with negative medians in most cuts. Author discloses survivorship bias, no transaction costs, single benchmark, and a fixed price-screen bug.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Weak-to-Moderate as evidence (unreviewed, one benchmark), but **it is the only analysis I found that tests the insider signal on a window that includes 2017–2026**, and its direction matters more than its rigor.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** This is the kind of finding you must not bury. If insider buys are in your 0–100 score, you owe the user a disclosure that the edge may be crisis-concentrated. The honest version: insider cluster buys are a *contextual* flag, and the product should not imply they predict the next 30 days.
**Note:** The vendor review (2iQ) explicitly asserts the opposite — that the signal is as relevant today as fifty years ago — and cites no study of decay. That asymmetry between vendor and independent framing is itself a finding.

### 1.6 13F / 13D / 13G institutional holdings

**CLAIM:** 13F data arrives up to 45 days after quarter end, and the delay is used deliberately by institutions — but for reasons (front-running protection, hiding voting power) that do not include copycat protection.
**SOURCE:** Agarwal, Mullally, Tang & Gerken / Musto et al. (Rodney L. White Center working paper 13-15), ["Why Do Institutions Delay Reporting Their Shareholdings? Evidence from Form 13F"](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/13-15.musto_.pdf). Working paper. 14 years of filings. Found: delays linked to fund outflows (front-running) and to merger/proposal record dates (voting), concentrated in index/quasi-index institutions for the flow channel and in hedge funds/active managers for the voting channel. No evidence that copycat risk drives lag. Average delays rose after 2006. Confidential-treatment (Rule 24b-2) holdings are withheld from the initial filing and, per Aragon, Hertzel & Shi (2013), carry significant abnormal returns — i.e. **the most informative positions are systematically the ones you cannot see on time.**
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate-to-Strong.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** 13F is structurally the wrong data for a product that proposes short-dated options. By the time a 13F is public, the position is 45–135 days old, the manager may have exited, and the best positions may be under confidential treatment. 13F is defensible as *narrative colour* ("three large funds added this quarter") and indefensible as a scoring input for a 30-day contract.

**CLAIM:** 13F cloning produced real historical outperformance in the best-ideas literature, but hedge-fund 13F abnormal returns declined after 2008 and copycat funds net of expenses have been found statistically indistinguishable from their targets.
**SOURCE:** [Quantpedia, "Alpha Cloning — Following 13F Filings"](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/alpha-cloning-following-13f-fillings) — **vendor/research aggregator**, summarizing Cohen, Polk & Silli "Best Ideas" (~1.6–2.1% per quarter over benchmarks; backtest ≈20.2%/yr, 1.26%/month alpha, 1991–2005, **gross of all costs**); Myers et al. (copycat funds net of expenses indistinguishable from underlying funds); Verbeek & Wang (copycats marginally outperform, better after the 2004 quarterly-disclosure rule); Amir-Ghassemi et al. (**hedge fund abnormal returns declined after 2008; best ideas showed no significant difference from aggregate positions**).
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Weak as presented (vendor summary, gross returns, no Sharpe/drawdown reported). The decay finding (Amir-Ghassemi) is the one to carry forward.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Exclude 13F from the numeric score. Display it, with the lag stated on screen.

### 1.7 LLMs applied to filings (2024–2026)

**CLAIM:** LLM-derived news/text embeddings deliver materially higher long-short Sharpe ratios than older encoder models — and, critically, chronologically clean models (trained only on pre-period text) perform as well as leaky ones, suggesting look-ahead bias is small in this application.
**SOURCE:** Hansen et al., "Chronologically Consistent Large Language Models," arXiv [2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206), 2025. Preprint. ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT vintages 1999–2024. News-based decile long-short, Jan 2008–Jul 2023: realtime ChronoBERT Sharpe 4.80, ChronoGPT 4.92, Llama 3.1 8B 4.90 (differences insignificant, p = 0.315/0.547); BERT 4.18, FinBERT 3.86, StoriesLM 3.42. Vintage performance follows an envelope rather than improving monotonically with later cutoffs.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate. The authors' leakage conclusion is careful but Llama's own Dec-2023 cutoff makes it an imperfect control, and they stress that leakage is model- and application-specific. **Sharpe ratios near 5 on a long-short news portfolio should be read as a research artifact of frictionless, equal-weighted, full-universe backtesting, not an achievable number.**
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Two things. First, FinBERT is *not* the strongest text encoder for return-relevant signal — a modern general LLM encoder beat it here. Second, and more important: **any backtest you run using today's LLM over historical filings is contaminated by the model knowing what happened.** This paper gives you the vocabulary to discuss it, and ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT give you a way to test it, but you cannot wave it away.

**CLAIM:** GPT-4 is competitive with or better than fine-tuned domain models on financial sentiment classification and financial numerical QA, but substantially worse on structured extraction (NER).
**SOURCE:** Li et al., "Are ChatGPT and GPT-4 General-Purpose Solvers for Financial Text Analytics?", arXiv [2305.05862](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05862). Preprint. Financial PhraseBank (50% agreement): GPT-4 5-shot 0.86 acc / 0.86 F1 vs FinBERT 0.86 / 0.84. PhraseBank (100%): GPT-4 0.97/0.97 vs FinBERT 0.97/0.95. FiQA: GPT-4 88.11 weighted F1 vs fine-tuned RoBERTa-large 87.09. TweetFinSent: GPT-4 71.95 acc vs RoBERTa-Twitter 72.30 — LLM slightly *worse* on social text. NER (FIN3 entity F1): GPT-4 20-shot 56.71, zero-shot 36.08, vs a CRF baseline at 82.70. FinQA: GPT-4 CoT 78.03 vs FinQANet 68.90 (human experts 91.16). ConvFinQA: GPT-4 zero-shot 76.48 vs 61.24.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate (preprint, benchmark-based, model versions now dated). The authors' own conclusion is that significant improvement is needed before an LLM can act as a trustworthy financial analyst agent.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Use an LLM for *judgment* tasks (classify this disclosure's tone, is this 8-K material) and a deterministic parser for *extraction* tasks (pull the numbers, identify the entity, segment the items). The NER gap — 57 vs 83 F1 — is exactly the failure mode that corrupts a numeric score, because a mis-extracted entity silently attributes a disclosure to the wrong company.

**CLAIM (NULL / CAUTIONARY):** A 2025 evaluation of LLMs summarizing 10-K filings found low inter-rater agreement, unquantified hallucinations, and model-to-model disagreement on the same filing — and offered no production-readiness verdict.
**SOURCE:** "Evaluating Large Language Models in Financial NLP," arXiv [2507.22936](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22936). Preprint. 21 filings, 7 large tech firms, Aug–Oct 2025. Krippendorff's α mostly near zero and sometimes negative across qualitative dimensions. Hallucinations and refusals were flagged and *removed* from metric totals rather than counted. Amazon's 2024 prompt produced the lowest cross-model agreement (mean 0.71, SD 0.078). Annotators were NLP/engineering researchers, not financial analysts.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Weak as a measurement (tiny sample, near-zero rater agreement) but **the near-zero agreement is itself the finding**: even experts cannot consistently judge whether an LLM's filing summary is good.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** If an LLM summary of a filing feeds a number in the 0–100 score, you have an unmeasurable error channel sitting upstream of a number the user will treat as precise. The mitigation is architectural: have the LLM output *extracted spans with citations into the filing*, score from the spans, and show the user the span. Never score from free prose.

## 2. News sentiment

**CLAIM:** Media pessimism predicts a next-day market decline of about 8 bp per standard deviation, which fully reverses within about a week; the gross strategy earned ~4.4 bp/day (~7.3%/yr) but the author did not establish post-cost profitability.
**SOURCE:** Tetlock, P.C., "Giving Content to Investor Sentiment: The Role of Media in the Stock Market," *Journal of Finance* 62(3), 2007, pp. 1139–1168. [PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3097/Tetlock_Media_Sentiment_JF.pdf). Peer-reviewed. WSJ "Abreast of the Market," 1984–1999. Next-day effect −8.1 bp per SD (−6.2 bp on the conservative 10am window); reversal of 6.8 bp over lags 2–5; net five-day effect −1.3 bp, not significantly different from zero. High |pessimism| predicts >1% higher next-day NYSE volume. Strategy positive in 12 of 15 years, but Tetlock states it is unclear whether it survives costs and taxes.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong (the finding), Strong (the negative conclusion on net profitability).
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** The canonical news-sentiment result is a **one-week mean-reverting noise-trader effect with no net five-day return**. That is a volatility and volume signal, not a direction signal. For an options product that is not useless — it argues for short-premium structures after extreme sentiment — but it is the opposite of "negative news ⇒ buy puts."

**CLAIM:** Firm-level negative news predicts lower earnings and a one-day price drift, but the drift is ~3 bp/day and the strategy dies at 9–10 bp round-trip transaction costs.
**SOURCE:** Tetlock, Saar-Tsechansky & Macskassy, "More Than Words: Quantifying Language to Measure Firms' Fundamentals," *Journal of Finance* 63(3), 2008, pp. 1437–1467. [PDF](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3096/More_Than_Words_tetlock.pdf). Peer-reviewed. DJNS + WSJ stories on S&P 500 firms, 1980–2004. Negative words predict lower SUE at the 99% level in all six specifications; SUE ~0.255 SD lower across a 4-SD move in negative words; ~half the economic impact of recent returns. Depressed earnings persist ≥4 quarters. DJNS next-day abnormal return −3.2 bp per SD; WSJ insignificant. Delayed reaction is ~17% of total for DJNS, ~7% for WSJ. Long-short alpha 9.2 bp/day (1980–94), 11.8 bp/day (1995–2004), positive in 21 of 25 years. **Profits disappear above ~9–10 bp round-trip costs.** Effect ~5x stronger for stories explicitly about earnings.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Two actionable items. (1) **Filter news by topic.** Earnings-related negative news is ~5x more informative than general negative news (−39.8 bp vs −8.6 bp same-day). A product that scores all news equally is diluting its own best signal. (2) The cost threshold — profits vanish at ~10 bp — is a death sentence for options implementation. 10 bp on a $100 stock is $0.10; a typical single-name option spread is 20–80× that in equity-equivalent terms.

**CLAIM:** Machine-readable news analytics measurably accelerate price incorporation, concentrating response in the first seconds — but only about a quarter of the vendor's sentiment information is in prices within 5 seconds, so the signal persists past HFT horizons.
**SOURCE:** "First to 'Read' the News: News Analytics and Algorithmic Trading," Federal Reserve International Finance Discussion Paper [1233](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/ifdp/files/ifdp1233.pdf). Fed working paper (institutional, not journal peer-reviewed). Uses RavenPack misclassification across software versions as an instrument. Correctly-classified high-relevance articles show 1.3 pp more of the 2-minute price reaction in the first 5 seconds (~10% relative to mean). Volume share in first 5s +0.5 pp, ~80% directional. False positives cause ~1 bp overreaction reversed in 30s–2min. Effect concentrated in press releases, where sentiment predicts direction 56% vs 53%. HFT activity +1.8 pp in the first 5 seconds, mostly liquidity-demanding and directional. Effective spreads and Amihud illiquidity both rise post-article. Returns from 5–120s (1.35 bp) exceed those in the first 5s (0.45 bp).
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate-to-Strong, and methodologically clever (causal identification via vendor error).
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** The good news for a retail product is that the news signal is *not* fully arbitraged in the first 5 seconds. The bad news is that the residual is measured in single basis points over two minutes. Neither horizon is reachable for a retail options user. The genuinely useful takeaway is the **press-release vs. reprint distinction**: timeliness and first-of-day status are what make a news item informative. Deduplicating reprints and identifying the originating item is a concrete engineering requirement.

**CLAIM (VENDOR, UNVERIFIED):** Commercial news-sentiment vendors continue to publish research asserting tradable alpha from their feeds.
**SOURCE:** RavenPack research pages, e.g. ["News Sentiment Everywhere: Trading Global Equities"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/351360244_News_Sentiment_Everywhere_Trading_Global_Equities) and ["The Timing Edge"](https://www.ravenpack.com/blog/the-timing-edge-new-research-unlocks-stronger-alpha-from-news-sentiment). **Vendor marketing, not peer-reviewed; no independent replication found.**
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** No credible independent evidence found that a commercially available news-sentiment feed carries net-of-cost alpha at retail scale. The peer-reviewed literature (Tetlock 2008) puts the cost threshold at ~10 bp, and the Fed paper shows the response is compressed into seconds.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Do not represent a purchased news-sentiment score to users as an edge. Represent it as context.

## 3. Social media sentiment

### 3.1 The foundational Twitter result does not replicate

**CLAIM:** Bollen, Mao & Zeng's "Twitter mood predicts the stock market" does not survive replication; the result appears driven by discarded data, a window chosen to minimize p-values, look-ahead normalization, and uncorrected multiple comparisons.
**SOURCE:** Lachanski, M. & Pav, S., "Shy of the Character Limit: 'Twitter Mood Predicts the Stock Market' Revisited," *Econ Journal Watch*, 2017. [PDF](https://econjwatch.org/file_download/1037/LachanskiPavSept2017.pdf) · [abstract](https://econjwatch.org/articles/shy-of-the-character-limit-twitter-mood-predicts-the-stock-market-revisited). Peer-reviewed (journal of replication/critique).
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong. Specific defects: 2007 and early-2008 Twitter data excluded without explanation (effect fails when included); the 28 Feb–3 Nov 2008 window gives the lowest p-values; BMZ's local z-score normalization uses future data and can manufacture a spurious negative mood/DJIA correlation from nothing; many models and lag structures tested without multiple-comparison correction; the implied Sharpe ratios exceed anything in the published market-timing literature; the POMS scoring table and tweet subsampling were never clarified and the original authors did not respond. Replication found no out-of-sample ARDL predictive power and no significant nonlinear outperformance (up-down accuracy 50–67.5%, with the 60% linear figure not significant). The authors concluded there is no credible evidence that raw Twitter mood forecasts daily index moves. Derwent Capital Markets, the hedge fund founded on the result, closed within about a year.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** **The single most-cited social-sentiment finding in existence is a false positive.** This should set the prior for every social-sentiment claim the product considers, including vendor claims. It is also the most honest thing the product could tell its users about social sentiment.

### 3.2 Firm-level social sentiment: a weak real effect, on a narrow margin

**CLAIM:** Aggregate pre-announcement Twitter opinion does predict earnings surprises and announcement returns, but the economic magnitude is ~9–30 bp over three days, and the effect requires a minimum crowd size.
**SOURCE:** Bartov, E., Faurel, L. & Mohanram, P., "Can Twitter Help Predict Firm-Level Earnings and Stock Returns?", *The Accounting Review* 93(3), 2018. [Final version PDF](https://www.bayes.citystgeorges.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0005/373154/Bartov-Faurel-Mohanram-July-18-2017-TAR-FINAL-VERSION.pdf) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2782236). Peer-reviewed. 869,733 cashtag tweets, 33,186 firm-quarters, 3,604 Russell 3000 firms, days −10 to −2, announcements Jan 2009–Dec 2012. SUE: OPI_BAYES 0.0275 (t = 2.84), OPI_VOCAB 0.3138 (t = 13.98). Three-day (−1,+1) abnormal return: OPI_BAYES 0.0599 (t = 3.69), OPI_VOCAB 0.2360 (t = 2.83). Economic size: 25th→75th percentile of OPI_BAYES ≈ 9.4 bp over three days; OPI_VOCAB ≈ 30.4 bp. Results significant only for firm-quarters with ≥5 distinct tweeting users. Stronger in poor information environments for OPI_VOCAB only; the OPI_BAYES interaction is insignificant.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong for existence, Weak for exploitability. Note the two measures disagree by ~3x in magnitude and disagree on the information-environment moderator — i.e. the result is measurement-sensitive.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** A real but tiny effect, concentrated around earnings, requiring a minimum crowd, measured on 2009–2012 data (pre-meme-era, pre-bot-era, and on an API that no longer exists at that price). Usable as an earnings-window feature with low weight; not usable as a general directional input.

**CLAIM:** StockTwits sentiment is strongly *contemporaneous* with returns but does not predict next-day returns unconditionally.
**SOURCE:** Divernois, M.-A. & Filipović, D., "StockTwits classified sentiment and stock returns," *Digital Finance* (Springer), 2023, DOI [10.1007/s42521-023-00102-z](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-023-00102-z). Peer-reviewed. ~90 million StockTwits messages on US/Canadian securities, Jan 2010–Mar 2020; logistic TF-IDF classifier at 85.9% out-of-sample accuracy. Daily polarity correlates ~0.1–0.3 with same-day returns for the six most-discussed 2019 tickers. The paper states plainly that polarity cannot predict next-day returns, which the authors read as consistent with market efficiency. An event study on 1,131 events across 19 tickers finds cumulative abnormal *polarity* distinguishes event types five days ahead while cumulative abnormal returns do not. Sentiment-sorted portfolios are described as outperforming/underperforming directionally, but no return, Sharpe, or significance figures are given, and the authors note dependence on a threshold multiplier and a 19-ticker universe. **Transaction costs are not addressed at all.**
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate. Large, clean dataset; the central negative finding (no next-day prediction) is credible. The positive event-anticipation finding is on 19 tickers with no cost analysis.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** StockTwits sentiment is a **concurrent mirror of price**, which means a product that shows it alongside a chart is showing the user a second copy of the chart. That is a real UX honesty problem: it will *look* predictive to a user scrolling history, because it moves with price.

### 3.3 WallStreetBets: the strongest negative evidence in this strand

**CLAIM:** A simple, implementable strategy following r/WallStreetBets buy and sell recommendations produces no risk-adjusted alpha at any horizon from one day to one year.
**SOURCE:** Buz & de Melo, "Will the reddit rebellion take you to the moon? Evidence from WallStreetBets," *Financial Markets and Portfolio Management*, 2022, DOI [10.1007/s11408-022-00415-w](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-022-00415-w). Peer-reviewed. Finding: not profitable on a risk-adjusted basis; horizons one day to one year all fail to produce alpha; the null holds across portfolio-formation methods. WSB activity *is* positively related to abnormal trading volume.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong (clean, directly relevant null result).
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Reddit/WSB sentiment predicts **attention and volume**, not returns. For an options product, that is actually a usable mapping — attention predicts volume predicts implied volatility — but the honest output is "this name is crowded and its options are likely expensive," which is an argument for *selling* premium, the reverse of what a retail user following WSB expects.

**CLAIM:** Meme-stock sentiment predictability is horizon- and source-dependent, with Google search volume the strongest signal (3–7 days), Bloomberg news second (7–14 days), and Twitter sentiment losing power after one trading day.
**SOURCE:** Li, J. & Li, Z., "Sentiment, social media and meme stock return predictability," *Review of Behavioral Finance* 18(2), 2026, pp. 97–116, DOI [10.1108/RBF-05-2025-0212](https://www.emerald.com/rbf/article-abstract/18/2/97/1336772/Sentiment-social-media-and-meme-stock-return) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4947010). Peer-reviewed, very recent. Two value-weighted meme indices from r/WallStreetBets ticker mentions; three sentiment measures scaled 0–100; daily data Jan 2021–Dec 2022; lags 1–14 days; robust to term spread, volatility, volume controls. Direction and magnitude of the coefficients are not stated in the abstract.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate. Peer-reviewed and recent, but the sample is two years that include the single most anomalous retail-trading episode in market history, and the abstract does not report effect signs or sizes — which makes it impossible to assess whether this is a momentum or a contrarian result.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Notable that **Google Trends beat both Twitter and news** here. Google Trends is free, has a public (if rate-limited and unofficial) interface, and carries none of Reddit's or X's licensing exposure. If the product wants a retail-attention feature, Trends is the cheapest legally cleanest proxy — and it may be the better one.

**CLAIM:** Crowd-sourced long-form opinion (Seeking Alpha) predicts returns and earnings surprises over months, with magnitudes around 0.33% abnormal return per percentage point of negative-word share over three months.
**SOURCE:** Chen, H., De, P., Hu, Y. & Hwang, B.-H., "Wisdom of Crowds: The Value of Stock Opinions Transmitted Through Social Media," *Review of Financial Studies* 27(5), 2014, pp. 1367–1403, DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhu001. [Working paper PDF](https://www.bhwang.com/pdf/wisdom-of-crowds.pdf). Peer-reviewed. 2005–2012, 7,000+ firms, 97,070 single-ticker articles plus ~460,000 comments within two days; LM negative-word share. Articles: ~−0.33% abnormal return per pp (t ≈ −2.0, days t+3 to t+60) with comments included; comments ~−0.19% (t ≈ −3.4). Long-short calendar-time spreads only ~2.2–2.6 bp/day. Earnings surprises ~0.23–0.27 points lower per pp (t ≈ −2.3 to −2.5) across 3,621 announcements; comment effect weaker and not robust. Effect stronger in the top tercile of comment volume (~0.51% per pp).
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate-to-Strong, with the authors' own caveats: they cannot rule out a "clout" channel where readers naively react; only single-ticker articles (~1/3 of content); possible survivorship from ex-post article removal; effects described by the authors as small in absolute terms; several results untabulated.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** The one social-media result with a *multi-month* horizon and a fundamentals link. But Seeking Alpha is a paywalled, ToS-protected property with no public API, which makes it the least obtainable of the sources. Noted as the signal you would most want and least be able to get.

### 3.4 Synthesis: predictive, contrarian, or noise?

Based on the above, the honest answer differs by name type and by what you are predicting:

| | Large-cap, liquid | Meme-adjacent / small-cap |
|---|---|---|
| **Predicting direction** | Essentially noise. StockTwits polarity is contemporaneous (Divernois & Filipović); WSB recommendations produce no alpha at any horizon (Buz & de Melo). Twitter opinion has a ~9–30 bp three-day earnings-window effect (Bartov et al.) that is below options frictions. | Weak and sample-dependent. Li & Li find 3–7 day Google-search and 7–14 day news effects on 2021–22 data; sign and size not reported. The one credible general test (WSB strategy) is null. |
| **Predicting volume/attention/IV** | Moderate and real. |tone| predicts volume (Tetlock 2007); WSB activity predicts abnormal volume (Buz & de Melo). | Strong and real. This is the meme-stock mechanism. |
| **Contrarian?** | Not established. The Tetlock 2007 reversal is a market-level, one-week noise-trader effect, not a firm-level contrarian rule. | Not established in the literature I found. The reversal narrative around meme episodes is widely believed and, as far as I can determine, not cleanly documented as a tradable rule. |

**No credible evidence found** that social sentiment is directionally predictive for large-cap optionable names at a horizon and magnitude an options trade could monetize.

## 4. Signal decay — the framing that governs everything

**CLAIM:** Published return predictors shrink about 26% out of sample and about 58% after publication; the incremental publication effect is roughly 32%.
**SOURCE:** McLean, R.D. & Pontiff, J., "Does Academic Research Destroy Stock Return Predictability?", *Journal of Finance* 71(1), 2016. [PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf) · [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2156623). Peer-reviewed. 97 predictors from 79 studies. Average monthly long-short: in-sample 0.582%, post-sample pre-publication 0.402%, post-publication 0.264%. Post-sample decline ~15.0 bp (~26%, treated as an upper bound on statistical bias); post-publication decline ~33.7 bp (~58%); implied publication effect ~32% (lower bound). Mechanism evidence: larger declines for higher in-sample returns; *smaller* declines for predictors concentrated in costly-to-arbitrage stocks (high idiosyncratic risk, low liquidity); post-publication increases in trading volume and short interest; correlations shift toward other published predictors.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Two hard consequences. (1) **Apply a shrinkage factor of roughly 0.4 to every published effect size before it enters the score.** A 50 bp/month published alpha should be modelled at ~20 bp. (2) The finding that decay is *smaller* in illiquid, hard-to-arbitrage stocks is a trap for an options product: those are exactly the names with the widest options spreads and thinnest open interest. The signals that survive are on the contracts you cannot trade.

**CLAIM:** Of 452 anomaly variables, about 65% fail at |t| ≥ 1.96 and about 82% fail at a multiple-testing threshold of |t| ≥ 2.78, under NYSE breakpoints and value-weighting.
**SOURCE:** Hou, K., Xue, C. & Zhang, L., "Replicating Anomalies," *Review of Financial Studies* 33(5), 2020, pp. 2019–2133. [PDF](https://global-q.org/uploads/1/2/2/6/122679606/houxuezhang2020rfs.pdf) · [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/33/5/2019/5236964). Peer-reviewed. Data Jan 1967–Dec 2016. Microcaps are ~3% of aggregate market cap but ~61% of stocks. Under equal-weighting with all-exchange breakpoints (maximum microcap weight), failure drops to 41.4% (single) / 52% (multiple) — i.e. **microcap-driven returns are what inflate published results.** Trading-frictions anomalies fail worst: 102 of 106 (96%) fail single tests. Even replicated anomalies are much smaller than originally reported. Notable failures include debt-to-market, the Piotroski F-score, and Fama-French operating profitability.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** The microcap result is directly load-bearing. A retail options product trades liquid optionable names — i.e. precisely the value-weighted, NYSE-breakpoint universe where failure rates are *highest*. Published anomaly evidence is systematically less applicable to this product's universe than to the literature's.

**CLAIM:** Standard significance thresholds are inappropriate given hundreds of tested factors; a new factor should clear |t| > 3.0, and most published findings in financial economics are likely false.
**SOURCE:** Harvey, C.R., Liu, Y. & Zhu, H., "…and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns," *Review of Financial Studies* 29(1), 2016, pp. 5–68. [PDF](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P118_and_the_cross.PDF) · [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/29/1/5/1843824). Peer-reviewed. 316 factors from 313 articles. Of 296 published significant factors, false-discovery counts: 158 under Bonferroni, 142 under Holm, 132 under BHY at 1%, 80 under BHY at 5%.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Strong (methodologically; the "most are false" conclusion is contested — see next).
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** Any in-house backtest run across many feature/parameter combinations needs a multiple-testing adjustment, not a t > 2 gate. If the product's scoring model is tuned by trying variants until the backtest looks good, the resulting score is a data-mined artifact with no out-of-sample claim.

**CLAIM (DISCONFIRMING — the decay literature is itself contested):** Publication bias in asset pricing is modest, accounting for only ~10–15% of in-sample mean returns, with a false discovery rate under 10%; the "most findings are false" framing confuses "many insignificant findings" with "mostly false findings."
**SOURCE:** Chen, A.Y. & Zimmermann, T., "Publication Bias in Asset Pricing Research," arXiv [2209.13623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13623); related: Chen, "Most claimed statistical findings in cross-sectional return predictability are likely true," arXiv [2206.15365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15365). Also relevant: Chen & Zimmermann, "Open Source Cross-Sectional Asset Pricing," [Fed FEDS 2021-037](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2021-037pap.pdf). Preprints/working papers by Fed economists; the Open Source Asset Pricing dataset is widely used.
**EVIDENCE STRENGTH:** Moderate. Their four supporting facts: replicability, out-of-sample persistence, t-stats well above 2.0, and weak cross-predictor correlations. Their critique of HXZ: only ~26% of HXZ's strategies were clearly significant in the original papers, many "failed replications" are ad-hoc variants, and microcaps explain only about a third of the failures. Their critique of HLZ: the |t| > 3.0 hurdle reflects conservatism in multiple-testing methods, not evidence that many results are false.
**IMPLICATION FOR THE APP:** The replication crisis is real but the pessimistic reading is not consensus. The defensible middle position for the product: published effects are mostly real but much smaller than advertised, and shrinkage (McLean & Pontiff's ~0.4) is better justified than wholesale dismissal. **Also: Chen & Zimmermann's Open Source Asset Pricing dataset is free and would let the product validate its own signals against 200+ published predictors rather than asserting novelty.**

### Scraping / legal risk map (risk mapping for a later legal strand — not legal advice)
- **CFAA has weakened as a tool against scraping public, non-authenticated data.** *Van Buren v. United States* (2021, 6–3) adopted a "gates-up-or-down" test: liability attaches to accessing areas you are not entitled to reach at all, not to misusing access you have. The Court expressly left open whether ToS alone can define "authorization." The Ninth Circuit in *hiQ v. LinkedIn* (2022, on remand) applied that reasoning to hold that public profiles have no gates to lift or lower.
- **But hiQ lost on contract.** In November 2022 a district court found hiQ had breached LinkedIn's User Agreement through automated scraping and fake accounts; the case settled in December 2022 with a permanent injunction, deletion of the scraped data, and a reported $500,000 payment. **The headline "scraping is legal" reading of hiQ is misleading: hiQ won the CFAA question and lost the case.**
- **The live risk vector is contract/ToS, not CFAA.** *Meta v. Bright Data* (N.D. Cal. 2024) dismissed Meta's CFAA claim but turned on a logged-in/logged-out distinction, with the court finding Meta's terms governed logged-in conduct and did not bar collecting public data while logged out. US courts remain split on browsewrap enforceability.
- **What has actually sunk scrapers:** fake accounts, logged-in automation, and defeating technical barriers. Those are the facts that produce losses.
- **Adjacent exposures:** copyright on article/post text; privacy law (GDPR/CCPA) where posts are personal data — "publicly available" is not a blanket exemption, as Clearview AI enforcement across jurisdictions (2020–2025) shows. The EU is more restrictive on contract-based scraping bars than the US (*Ryanair v. PR Aviation*, CJEU 2015).
- **Open questions for counsel:** (1) Does ingesting X or Stocktwits content into a paid B2C product create contract exposure even where CFAA does not apply? (2) Does Reddit's post-March-2027 posture make any Reddit-derived aggregator a supply-chain risk worth avoiding entirely? (3) Does deriving a *numeric score* from licensed news and discarding the text satisfy standard no-redistribution clauses? (4) Does using an MCP endpoint intended for an end user's AI assistant as product backend infrastructure constitute an unagreed commercial use?
- Sources for the above: [National Law Review on the Ninth Circuit's post-Van Buren hiQ opinion](https://natlawreview.com/article/taking-cue-supreme-court-s-van-buren-decision-ninth-circuit-releases-new-opinion); [iswebscrapinglegal.com case-law survey](https://iswebscrapinglegal.com/blog/web-scraping-case-law/); [apiserpent.com 2026 review](https://apiserpent.com/blog/is-scraping-linkedin-legal-2026) (**vendor blog — commercial interest in a permissive reading**); [California Lawyers Association](https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/ninth-circuit-holds-data-scraping-is-legal-in-hiq-v-linkedin/).

## 7. DISCONFIRMING EVIDENCE

Required to be non-empty. It is not short.

1. **The most famous social-sentiment result in finance is a false positive.** Bollen, Mao & Zeng's Twitter-mood paper fails replication on discarded data, a p-value-minimizing window, look-ahead normalization, and uncorrected multiple comparisons; the hedge fund built on it closed within about a year ([Lachanski & Pav 2017](https://econjwatch.org/file_download/1037/LachanskiPavSept2017.pdf)).
2. **A direct, peer-reviewed test of the exact thing this product would do with Reddit found nothing.** Following WSB buy/sell recommendations produces no risk-adjusted alpha at horizons from one day to one year, across portfolio-formation methods ([Buz & de Melo 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-022-00415-w)).
3. **StockTwits sentiment does not predict next-day returns** — the authors say so plainly, on 90 million messages over a decade ([Divernois & Filipović 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42521-023-00102-z)).
4. **Loughran & McDonald's own 10-K negative-word strategy produced no statistically significant alpha**, and their R² was ~2.4–2.6%. The authors of the field's foundational sentiment paper warn against treating text as a key to returns.
5. **The news-sentiment effect is net-zero over a week at the market level** (Tetlock 2007: −8.1 bp day 1, +6.8 bp over days 2–5, net −1.3 bp insignificant) and **dies above ~10 bp round-trip costs** at the firm level (Tetlock et al. 2008). Options frictions are an order of magnitude above that threshold.
6. **Insider cluster buys may be a 2008–2009 artifact.** An independent 2006–2026 Form 4 study finds ~94% of the excess-return sum in 2008–09 and a slightly negative post-2017 average ([donttrade](https://donttrade.substack.com/p/the-insider-buying-edge-was-mostly), unreviewed). The vendor literature asserts the opposite and cites no decay study.
7. **Most insider trades carry no information at all** — routine traders' abnormal returns are ≈ zero and they are over half of all insider trades (Cohen/Malloy/Pomorski 2012). A naive insider badge is noise dressed as signal.
8. **65% of 452 anomalies fail to replicate at t ≥ 1.96, and 82% fail at a multiple-testing threshold** — with failure *worst* in the value-weighted, large-cap universe this product trades (Hou/Xue/Zhang 2020).
9. **Published predictability falls ~58% after publication** (McLean & Pontiff 2016), and the predictors that survive are concentrated in illiquid, hard-to-arbitrage stocks — i.e. the ones with unusable options.
10. **LLMs are worse than fine-tuned models at structured extraction** (NER F1 56.7 vs 82.7) and worse than a fine-tuned model on social-media financial sentiment (TweetFinSent 71.95 vs 72.30) ([arXiv 2305.05862](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.05862)). The "just use GPT" architecture is not uniformly better.
11. **Experts cannot agree on whether an LLM's filing summary is good.** Krippendorff's α near zero, sometimes negative, across qualitative dimensions; hallucinations were flagged and excluded rather than counted ([arXiv 2507.22936](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2507.22936)).
12. **The LLM news-sentiment Sharpe ratio declined within its own two-year sample**, from 6.54 (2021Q4) to 3.68 (2022) to 2.33 (2023), which the authors read as suggestive of decay from wider LLM adoption ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07619)). Their strategy is also unprofitable at 20 bp round-trip costs, and they note cost minimization may be infeasible for unsophisticated investors — this product's exact users.
13. **13F copycat funds net of expenses are statistically indistinguishable from the funds they copy**, and hedge-fund 13F abnormal returns declined after 2008 (Myers et al.; Amir-Ghassemi et al., via [Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/alpha-cloning-following-13f-fillings)).
14. **Disconfirming the pessimists, too:** Chen & Zimmermann estimate publication bias at only ~10–15% of in-sample returns with an FDR under 10%, and argue HXZ misclassified ad-hoc variants as failed replications ([arXiv 2209.13623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13623)). The replication crisis is contested in both directions; this report's ~0.4 shrinkage is a middle position, not a settled constant.
15. **The data is going away faster than the signals are decaying.** Reddit's public API closes March 2027; Stocktwits' developer registration is closed; X's affordable tier offers only a 7-day search window. Some of the signals above are now unbuildable regardless of whether they work.

## 8. OPEN QUESTIONS

**Signal / methodology**
1. Has Lazy Prices been tested out of sample on 2015–2026? Its sample ends in 2014 and it is now a famous, widely-known signal with a 2020 publication date — McLean & Pontiff predict ~58% shrinkage from exactly this.
2. Does the Lazy Prices signal survive in the liquid-optionable universe specifically, value-weighted? The 188 bp figure is a section subset; the 58 bp VW figure is the right starting point, but neither is restricted to names with usable options chains.
3. What is the Lazy Prices signal's behaviour under the SEC's own disclosure modernization? If filing-language change is partly a boilerplate-update artifact, regulatory changes to required disclosure create false positives in known years.
4. Does any filing or sentiment signal predict **implied-vs-realized volatility** rather than direction? This is the right question for an options product and I found almost no literature on it. File size → post-filing volatility (LM 2014) is the one concrete lead.
5. For the insider signal, what does a properly specified routine/opportunistic classification show on 2017–2026 data, value-weighted, net of costs? The only post-2017 evidence I found is an unreviewed blog study using a naive cluster definition.
6. Li & Li (2026) do not report the sign of their meme-stock sentiment coefficients in the abstract. Momentum or contrarian? This determines whether a social-sentiment feature should be positively or negatively weighted, and it is unresolved.
7. Why did Google Trends beat both Twitter and news sentiment in Li & Li? If search attention dominates platform sentiment, the entire social-scraping effort may be the wrong investment.

**LLM engineering**
8. What does it actually cost to run an LLM over the filing corpus at product scale? The "From Text to Alpha" authors restricted themselves to the S&P 100 *because of extraction cost*. A product covering 3,000 tickers with 10-K + 10-Q + 8-K history faces a cost wall that paper ran into at 100 tickers.
9. How do you measure hallucination rate in a filing-extraction pipeline when expert raters cannot agree on output quality (α ≈ 0)? Span-level citation checking is the obvious answer but I found no established benchmark.
10. Does the ChronoBERT/ChronoGPT approach ([arXiv 2502.21206](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.21206)) make an honest backtest of an LLM-based filing signal feasible for a small team, or is the engineering cost prohibitive?
11. What is the latency budget? If filing analysis takes an LLM 30–120 seconds per document and the user expects a ticker lookup in under 3 seconds, the architecture must be precomputed-and-cached, which changes the ingestion cost model entirely.

**Data and legal (for the respective strands)**
12. Current pricing and terms for Benzinga, Polygon news, NewsAPI.org, and Alpha Vantage NEWS_SENTIMENT — **unverified in this report** and required before any planning.
13. Operating status, pricing, and upstream licensing posture of Quiver Quantitative, Swaggy Stocks, and similar social-sentiment resellers — **unverified**, and newly urgent given Reddit's shutdown timeline.
14. Is there any licensed, affordable path to Reddit data for a small B2C product after March 2027, or is the category simply closed below enterprise budgets?
15. Does X's February 2026 pay-as-you-go model make per-ticker sentiment economically feasible at retail scale? At ~$0.005 per third-party post read, 500 posts per ticker per day across 500 tickers is ~$1,250/day.
16. Does using Stocktwits' MCP endpoint as product infrastructure (rather than as an end user's own assistant connector) breach its terms? Counsel question.
17. Does deriving and storing only a numeric score from licensed news, discarding the text, satisfy standard no-redistribution clauses? Counsel question.

## 10. Three design consequences, stated plainly

1. **The score should be built mostly from EDGAR.** It is the only source in this strand that is free, legally clean, bulk-downloadable, and carries the best-evidenced signal (filing-language change). Social sentiment is simultaneously the weakest-evidenced and the least obtainable input. That is an unusually clean build-vs-skip decision.
2. **Most of these signals predict volatility and volume, not direction.** |News tone| → volume (Tetlock 2007). WSB activity → abnormal volume (Buz & de Melo). 10-K file size → post-filing volatility (LM 2014). For an options product this is a gift, not a disappointment — it maps onto strategy selection and premium-buying-vs-selling. But it means the "bullish/bearish/neutral" classifier should not be fed by these inputs, and the product's structure should reflect that separation.
3. **Every effect size in this report is below the frictions of a retail single-name options trade.** The largest honest number here — Lazy Prices value-weighted, ~58 bp/month gross, before the ~0.4 shrinkage — is roughly one month of signal against one option spread. The product's defensible value proposition is therefore *research synthesis and disclosure* — showing the user what the filings, insiders, and crowd actually say, with the evidence quality labelled — rather than *alpha generation*. A 0–100 score that implies the latter is a claim the literature in this strand does not support.
